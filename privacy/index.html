Billion Beers

Privacy Policy

Last updated 7 October 2026

Billion Beers is an app for logging the beers you drink and sharing them with friends. This policy explains what it collects, why, and what you can do about it. It is written to be read rather than to cover us, so if anything here is unclear, ask.

The short version. We collect what the app needs to work: your account details, the beers you log, and the photos you take of them. We do not sell your data, we do not show advertising, and there are no tracking or analytics tools in the app. You can delete your account, and everything in it, from inside the app at any time.

Who is responsible

Billion Beers is run by Jake Cripps as an individual, not a registered company. For anything about your data — a question, a correction, a copy, or a deletion — email billionsofbeers@gmail.com.

You have to be 18 or over

This app is about alcohol, so it is for adults only. We ask for your date of birth when you sign up and refuse accounts for under-18s. We do not knowingly collect anything from children. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and we will remove it.

What we collect

WhatWhy
Email address and password To create your account and sign you in. Passwords are hashed by our authentication provider and are never visible to us.
Username, display name, profile picture So friends can find you and your posts have a name on them. These are visible to other people using the app.

Comments, likes and reports To show conversation under posts, and to act on anything reported.

There is no advertising in Billion Beers, no third-party analytics, and no tracking across other apps or websites. We do not build advertising profiles and we do not sell or rent your data to anyone, ever.

Who else sees it

A few services process data on our behalf so the app can function. They act on our instructions and are not permitted to use your data for their own purposes.

  • Supabase — stores the database, your account and your photos. Hosted in the EU (Ireland).
  • Railway — runs the server the app talks to.
  • Google Cloud Vision — every photo you upload is sent for automatic checking before it is stored, both to detect explicit content and to read the beer's label so we can identify it. Google does not use these images to train its models.
  • Google Places — when you search for pubs nearby, your approximate location is sent so results can be returned.
  • Anthropic — generates background descriptions of beers. Only the beer and brewery name are sent. None of your personal data is.
  • Apple — distributes the app through TestFlight and the App Store, and tells us about crashes.

We will also disclose data if the law requires it, or to protect someone's safety. If that ever happens we will tell you unless we are legally prevented from doing so.

What other people can see

Your display name, username, profile picture, the beers you log and their photos are visible to other people using the app. If you turn on Private Profile in Settings, only accepted friends can see your beers. Your email address, date of birth and exact coordinates are never shown to other users.

Posts from public accounts appear in the app's global Live feed.

Keeping it civil

Every uploaded photo is scanned automatically and explicit material is refused before it is stored. You can report any post, and you can block any person, which hides you from each other entirely. Reports are read by a human. Accounts that abuse the app are removed.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law you can ask us to give you a copy of your data, correct it, delete it, or stop using it in a particular way. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Deleting your account

Settings → Delete Account, inside the app. That removes your profile, your beers, your photos, your comments and your messages. It cannot be undone. Beers you logged are removed from the global count at the same time.

If you would rather we did it, email billionsofbeers@gmail.com and we will action it within 30 days.

How long we keep things

Your account and its contents are kept until you delete them. Reports are kept for two years after they are dealt with, so repeated abuse can be recognised. Backups are kept for 30 days, so deleted data can persist in a backup for up to a month before it is overwritten.

Security

Everything travels over HTTPS. Passwords are hashed, never stored in plain text, and never visible to us. Access to the database is restricted to the people running the app. No system is perfectly secure, and we will not pretend otherwise — but if a breach affects you, we will tell you.

Where your data is held

Your data is stored in the European Union (Ireland). Some of the services above are run by companies based in the United States, which means data may be transferred there under appropriate safeguards.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects you, we will tell you in the app before it takes effect. The date at the top always reflects the current version.
